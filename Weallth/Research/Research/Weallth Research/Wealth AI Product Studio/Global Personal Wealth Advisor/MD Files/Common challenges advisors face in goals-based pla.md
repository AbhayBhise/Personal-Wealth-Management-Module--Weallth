# Common challenges advisors face in goals-based planning implementation

Based on industry research and expert analysis, here are the **common challenges advisors face in goals-based planning implementation**:

## Challenge 1: Clients Can't Articulate Their Goals

**The Problem:** When asked "what are your goals?", many clients answer "we don't really know" because they've never thought deeply about it before. Clients struggle to articulate their needs clearly and may not fully understand their own financial situation.[^1][^2]

**Research Finding:** A Morningstar study found that **73% of clients changed at least one of their original goals** when shown a master list of common financial goals, and **26% changed their number one goal entirely**.[^3]

**Solutions:**

- Use goal discovery frameworks with pre-defined goal categories to prompt thinking
- Conduct multiple conversations rather than expecting goals in one meeting[^3]
- Start by exploring possibilities before asking clients to commit to specific goals[^2]

***

## Challenge 2: Unrealistic Client Expectations

**The Problem:** Some clients have goals that are "wildly inappropriate" for their actual resources (assets, income, savings), making them totally unrealistic. Crafting a plan just projects catastrophic failure, forcing difficult conversations.[^2]

**Impact:** Advisors become "bearers of bad news" when showing clients their goals are unattainable, damaging trust and engagement.[^2]

**Solutions:**

- Use interactive software to explore trade-offs collaboratively before finalizing goals
- Show possibilities first, then help clients select realistic goals[^2]
- Frame conversations around prioritization rather than rejection of goals

***

## Challenge 3: Analysis Paralysis \& Iteration Overload

**The Problem:** The goals-based process can get "mired down in countless iterations of alternative what-if plans," culminating in massive documents that clients never read again after presentation.[^2]

**Root Cause:** When clients aren't certain they've selected the "right" goals, every scenario generates more questions and more alternative plans.[^2]

**Solutions:**

- Limit scenario presentations to 2-3 meaningful alternatives
- Use real-time interactive planning instead of back-and-forth offline analysis[^2]
- Focus on key trade-offs rather than exhaustive permutations

***

## Challenge 4: Disconnect Between Planning \& Implementation

**The Problem:** There's a significant gap between the holistic financial planning process and actual portfolio implementation. Manual oversight required to align investments with changing goals isn't scalable.[^4]


| Planning Phase | Implementation Gap |
| :-- | :-- |
| Goals identified | Portfolios not structured by goal |
| Risk assessed per goal | Single portfolio with blended risk |
| Timeline established | No automatic rebalancing toward goal dates |

**Solutions:**

- Use integrated technology that connects planning to execution[^4]
- Implement goals-based account structures (separate accounts per goal)
- Automate rebalancing and glide paths toward goal timelines

***

## Challenge 5: Technology \& Operational Silos

**The Problem:** Legacy wealth management systems create silos across managed account programs, making true goals-based investment management extremely difficult. Key challenges include:[^5]

- **Asset location** — Assets scattered across multiple accounts
- **Tax optimization** — No unified view for tax-loss harvesting across goals
- **End-to-end supervision** — Disconnected systems for planning vs. trading
- **Risk capacity vs. tolerance mismatch** — Different assessments not reconciled[^5]

**Impact:** Advisors spend more time on operational tasks rather than building relationships and giving holistic advice.[^5]

**Solutions:**

- Invest in integrated platforms connecting planning, portfolio management, and trading
- Consolidate client data into unified household views
- Automate compliance and reporting across goal-based accounts

***

## Challenge 6: Behavioral \& Emotional Client Reactions

**The Problem:** Even after clients buy into goals-based planning conceptually, they may still react emotionally to market fluctuations. Common behavioral issues include:[^6]


| Bias | Impact on Goals-Based Planning |
| :-- | :-- |
| **Panic selling** | Clients abandon long-term goals during downturns [^7] |
| **Availability bias** | Recent events override long-term planning [^6] |
| **Fear-based goals** | Goals rooted in anxiety trigger outsized stress responses [^7] |
| **Impatience** | Premature withdrawal when results aren't immediate [^8] |

**Solutions:**

- Reframe goals around positive aspirations rather than fear[^7]
- Create goal-specific progress tracking to maintain motivation[^8]
- Provide behavioral coaching during volatility using goal context
- Show progress toward goals, not just portfolio performance[^9]

***

## Challenge 7: Building Trust \& Credibility

**The Problem:** Goals-based planning requires deep personal conversations about life aspirations, which demands significant client trust. Advisors must navigate sensitive topics like mortality (legacy planning), family dynamics, and financial fears.[^1]

**Barrier:** Clients may be reluctant to share true priorities until trust is established, leading to incomplete goal discovery.[^10]

**Solutions:**

- Build rapport before diving into financial data collection[^11]
- Demonstrate expertise through educational content
- Show empathy and active listening during discovery sessions
- Follow through consistently on commitments[^1]

***

## Challenge 8: Scalability for Large Client Bases

**The Problem:** Goals-based planning is inherently more personalized and time-intensive than traditional approaches, creating scalability challenges for advisors managing many clients.[^10][^4]

**Time Demands:**

- Multiple discovery conversations per client
- Custom scenario analysis for each client's unique goals
- Ongoing monitoring of multiple goals per household
- Regular progress updates and plan revisions

**Solutions:**

- Leverage technology for automated progress tracking and alerts
- Use templated workflows for common goal types
- Segment clients by complexity and service model
- Automate routine communications (quarterly updates, milestone alerts)[^12]

***

## Challenge 9: Educating Clients on the Approach

**The Problem:** Many clients expect advisors to focus on investment returns and benchmark performance, not life goals. Shifting the conversation requires education about why goals-based planning delivers better outcomes.[^1]

**Solutions:**

- Explain the research showing higher satisfaction rates (84% vs. 67%)[^9]
- Use visual tools demonstrating goal progress vs. benchmark comparisons
- Share case studies of successful goal achievement[^13]
- Position yourself as "family CFO" rather than investment manager[^14]

***

## Summary: Challenge vs. Solution Matrix

| Challenge | Primary Solution |
| :-- | :-- |
| Clients can't articulate goals | Multi-conversation discovery with goal menus |
| Unrealistic expectations | Interactive scenario exploration before commitment |
| Analysis paralysis | Limit to 2-3 scenarios, use real-time tools |
| Planning-implementation gap | Integrated technology platforms |
| Technology silos | Unified wealth management systems |
| Emotional reactions | Behavioral coaching with goal-based framing |
| Trust building | Relationship-first approach before data gathering |
| Scalability | Automation + client segmentation |
| Client education | Research-backed value proposition |
